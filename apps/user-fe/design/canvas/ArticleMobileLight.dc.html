Damp-proof your walls before the monsoon

Paint can’t stop water that is already getting in. Find the source, fix it, let the wall dry, then seal it. Here is the order of work we suggest at the counter.

1. Find where the water gets in

  • Cracks in the roof terrace, and drains blocked with leaves or plastic.
  • Gaps around window frames, pipes and the joint where the parapet meets the slab.
  • Rising damp at the bottom of ground-floor walls.
  • A leaking bathroom or water tank on the floor above.

Quick check: if the patch is wettest just after rain, the water is coming from outside. If it stays wet near the floor all year, it is rising damp.

2. Fix the source first

Fill cracks, clear and re-slope the terrace drains, seal around frames and pipes, and repair plumbing. Waterproof paint over an active leak only hides it for a season.

3. Let the wall dry

Wait for a run of dry days. To test, tape a square of plastic sheet to the wall overnight. If there are water drops under it in the morning, the wall is still too wet to coat.

4. Prepare the surface

  1. Scrape off loose and flaking paint.
  2. Brush away the white salty powder (efflorescence) with a dry stiff brush.
  3. Wash off fungus and moss, then let the wall dry again.

5. Seal, then paint

Apply a damp-proof primer or waterproof coating, following the drying time on the can. Then finish with two coats of exterior emulsion.

Plan ahead Finish outside work before the rains The monsoon usually arrives in June. Aim to finish sealing and painting outside walls by late May.
